Choose postal packaging for small parcels, gift orders and jewellery dispatch
Different products need different levels of protection and different outer dimensions. For boxed jewellery, a postal box usually gives the best support, especially when sending ring boxes, necklace boxes, bracelet boxes or earrings in presentation boxes. For flatter products such as gift cards, vouchers or cards, board-backed and large letter packaging can help keep contents straight while controlling mailing costs.
If you are sending soft-packed or low-profile items, mailing bags and postal bags can be a practical option where rigidity is less important. For delicate items or mixed orders, padded options add extra cushioning without needing separate void fill for every parcel. The right choice comes down to the product inside, the protection needed and the outer size required for your chosen postal service.
Postal boxes, mailing bags and letterbox packaging
Postal boxes and postage boxes are the strongest option for many jewellery and gift shipments. They work well for gift boxes and bags , multi-item orders and products that need a more rigid outer pack. They are also useful when you want a cleaner, more consistent look on arrival, especially for ecommerce orders where presentation still matters after transit.
Mailing bags and postal bags are better suited to lighter, less fragile products or to items already protected inside another pack. Letterbox boxes and large letter boxes are useful when you want to stay within letterbox-friendly or large letter dimensions. That can help reduce postage costs and missed deliveries for flatter orders, while still giving more structure than a standard bag.

Which postal packaging type is right for your products?
Use rigid postal boxes for jewellery boxes, small gift sets and accessories that need stronger edge and crush protection. Choose board-backed formats for flat items such as gift cards, vouchers, printed inserts or slim accessory packs that need to stay flat in the post. Padded options are a good fit for delicate items that are not boxed, or for added cushioning around small high-value pieces.
When comparing formats, focus on protection level, packing speed, postal compatibility and final packed size. A box is usually the safest choice for jewellery presentation boxes, while mailing bags and large letter packaging work best when the product is already flat, light or protected inside another pack.
Letterbox boxes and large letter packaging for cost-efficient shipping
Letterbox boxes and large letter packaging are useful for businesses sending flatter orders that do not need a deeper parcel format. They can work well for earrings on cards, slim pouches, vouchers, gift cards, flat-packed accessories and selected small gift sets, provided the complete pack meets the outer dimensions needed for large letter or letterbox delivery.
This format can help lower postage costs and make delivery more convenient for the recipient. It also supports a more predictable pack size for repeat orders. Before ordering, check the dimensions of the product, any inner box or insert, the final assembled depth and the packed weight against your chosen postal service. If you are sending a presentation box, a standard postal box is often the safer choice.

Postal packaging FAQs
If you are comparing postage packaging for jewellery, gift retail or ecommerce dispatch, these are the questions we hear most often from trade buyers.
What postal packaging is best for sending jewellery boxes?
For most jewellery boxes, a rigid postal box is the safest option. It gives better protection against crushing and helps keep ring boxes, necklace boxes, bracelet boxes and earring boxes stable in transit. If the item is especially delicate or there is spare space around the inner pack, add tissue, inserts or protective packing material for a closer fit.
Which option should I choose for gift cards, vouchers or flat-packed items?
Board-backed and large letter packaging are usually the best fit for flat items such as gift cards, vouchers, cards and slim accessory packs. They help keep contents flat and can be more cost-effective than a parcel box when the final outer dimensions stay within large letter limits.
When should I use a postal box instead of a mailing bag?
Choose a postal box when the product is boxed, delicate, presentation-sensitive or part of a multi-item order. A mailing bag is better for lighter, low-profile items that already have enough protection inside their own packaging. If arrival condition and shape retention matter, a box is usually the stronger choice.

What other shipping supplies should I order with postal packaging?
Many businesses order labels, stickers, tissue paper, ribbon, inserts and protective packing materials alongside their postal packaging. Ordering outer packaging and presentation extras together helps keep packing more consistent and saves time when you are replenishing stock for regular dispatch.
