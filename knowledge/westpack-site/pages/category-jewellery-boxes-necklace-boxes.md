Choose the right necklace boxes by size, depth and box style
Dimensions matter more with necklace packaging than with many other jewellery boxes. A box that is too short, too shallow or fitted with the wrong insert can let a chain move, kink or sit awkwardly. Slimline necklace boxes are a good fit for flatter jewellery presentation, fine chains and pieces that do not need much lift. They also save space in drawers, on shelves and in ecommerce packing stations.
Deep necklace or pendant boxes are better when the piece has a larger drop, layered construction or a more substantial centre feature. They suit statement necklaces, larger pendants and presentations where you want more depth between the lid and the jewellery. Hinged boxes work well for counter presentation, while matchbox, lid-and-base and shoulder boxes give you different options for opening style, storage and merchandising. If you are comparing necklace box suppliers in the UK, this level of fit detail can make a real difference to returns, packing time and consistency.
Necklace gift boxes, pendant boxes and inserts explained
A full necklace box is usually the better choice when you need to present the whole line of the piece, support a longer chain or show a complete set-up on an insert. A pendant box is often sufficient when the focus is a smaller pendant on a fine chain and you do not need the same length or spread. Keeping that distinction clear helps avoid over-ordering larger boxes where a compact pendant format would do the job.
Insert and pad choice also affects both fit and presentation. Necklace-specific inserts can help hold delicate chains in place, keep pendants centred and separate layered pieces more neatly. For chokers and wider statement styles, a broader interior fit can improve how the jewellery sits when opened. Set boxes need enough space and the right internal layout if they are carrying matching pieces together. Material options such as card, leatherette, velvet, suede and textured finishes let you match the presentation to your range, while colour choices help with merchandising by collection, season or price tier. Necklace boxes FAQs
These are some of the most common buying questions we hear from jewellery businesses choosing necklace packaging.
What size necklace box do I need for different necklace lengths and styles?
Start with the jewellery type, chain length, drop and how you want the piece to sit when opened. Slimline boxes work well for fine chains and flatter presentation, while deeper formats suit larger pendants, layered pieces and statement necklaces. Check the internal dimensions and insert layout carefully, especially if you need space for a wider choker or a necklace set.
What is the difference between a necklace box and a pendant box?
A necklace box is made to present and support the full piece, often with more length or a necklace-specific insert to hold the chain in place. A pendant box is usually more compact and better suited to smaller pendants on fine chains where a full necklace format is not necessary. Choosing the right format helps control packaging cost and improves fit.
Are there wholesale necklace boxes available in bulk quantities?
Yes. Westpack offers wholesale necklace boxes in trade-friendly pack sizes, with clear quantity visibility to help you plan bulk purchases for retail stores, online orders and repeat stock replenishment. This makes it easier to compare pack-level pricing and order consistently across your range.
Can I order branded necklace boxes with my logo?
Yes. Branded necklace boxes with logo printing are available for businesses that want more consistent presentation across retail, gifting and ecommerce orders. Branding can also be coordinated with related packaging such as pouches, tissue paper, ribbon, carrier bags and other branded packaging.
Which necklace box styles are best for ecommerce and postal orders?
Postal-friendly styles are usually the better choice for ecommerce because they take up less space in fulfilment and can be easier to pack consistently. Slimmer matchbox or compact lid-and-base formats are often useful for flatter pieces, while deeper boxes may be needed for larger pendants or statement styles. The best option depends on product depth, insert fit and the outer shipping format you use.
