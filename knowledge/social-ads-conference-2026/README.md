## 1. Normal — scaling across markets (9:30)

Presumably Normal, the Danish retail chain, on running social across its countries.

- Versioned content does not work: it costs credibility ("versioneret content virker ikke →
  troværdighed").
- UGC across markets, through partnerships. Organic feel.
- People buy from a local sender ("køber lokal afsender").
- UGC versus partner:

  | UGC | Partner |
  |---|---|
  | Brand as the sender | Credibility |
  | Production capacity | Social proof |
  | Local recognition | Micro-creators are the way forward — with a clear angle |
  | Creative variance | |

- "Ikke samme modtagere" — not the same recipients (context not noted).

**For Westpack**

- We version a great deal: Duplicate & translate into 13–18 markets, the Trustpilot carousel
  template, DE/FR/IT voiceover videos. The talk's claim is that translated content loses to a
  local sender. Treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a rule; our template work stays.
- Our own data points the same way as the UGC column: the JOYO video won as a Westpack-identity
  ad (≈920 kr per new customer) and was weak as a partnership ad (`partner-ad-permissions-2026-11`
  memory). Local customer content with **Westpack as the sender** is the form that worked.
- For a B2B brand, micro-creators are existing customers in the partner programme (Joya by Luz,
  JOYO), never outside influencers. Rights must be explicit: Muschelgold gave none.
- Our reading of "not the same recipients", not the speaker's: a jewellery brand's followers are
  consumers, not packaging buyers, so the partner's identity may lend trust to the wrong people.

## 2. Category entry points and the creator brief

- Category entry points (CEPs) are concrete situations that send people hunting in the category.
- The market now versus later: now → hardcore product; later → UGC / partnership.
- Brief the **situation the person is in, not the target group.**
- A clear link to the brand already in the hook.
- A situation brief to creators instead of a product brief gives better hooks.
- The link to Westpack is carried by product, colour, font and uniform (distinctive assets).
- The recipe: **1 CEP per video → situation first → brand early → the right sender → the product
  as the answer.**
- The speaker's creator programme runs in a Claude web app: creators sign up, products come from
  the PIM, corrections are made there and rights are held there.

**For Westpack**

- Candidate Westpack CEPs, to be validated with the team, not taken as found: opening a shop or a
  new collection; the Christmas rush and gift wrapping; webshop orders that must look good on
  unboxing; a new logo or rebrand; a fair, market or pop-up; running low on boxes before a peak.
- Each video takes one situation, shows Westpack within the first seconds and lands on "jewellery
  boxes with your logo" (the category, not the series — `ad-copy-sells-the-category-not-the-series`).
- Westpack has no written list of distinctive brand assets (colour, typeface, uniform, recurring
  devices). The "brand early" rule needs one.
- A partner portal in biblen (sign-up, product choice, corrections, a rights register) is a
  candidate, not built. A rights register would have made the Muschelgold case checkable.

## 3. Copywriter's guide to B2B (partial — read through the paper)

- Not neat and polished, or you look like everyone else. The Von Restorff effect: the odd one out
  is remembered. Dare to be a little crooked.
- The audience thinks about itself: what's in it for me. The "so what?" test, possibly asked
  several times, finds the angle.
- The cheat code: use the audience's own words (uncertain reading).
- The good hook is relevant, catches attention, pokes a little and makes you want to read on.
- The pancake test (content not readable).
- Fluffy bullshit is found by reversing the sentence. If nobody would ever say the opposite, the
  sentence says nothing.

**The four copywriting basics**

- "Du", not "man".
- Active verbs.
- Write light and lively.
- Human language.
- Don't be a "blå Klovborg" — the user: the most ordinary everyday sliced cheese. Don't be the
  generic, interchangeable option.

**For Westpack:** "du not man", passive voice and the reverse-the-sentence test can be checked
mechanically. They are candidates for the Email check and the subject-line writer's rules, not yet
implemented. They sit alongside the existing rules (never talk down to the customer, claims only
from the site).

## 6. Holst Mouritsen — "Det stærkeste måleapparat" and the measurement ladder

The user found this talk "ultra værdifuldt". The speaker could not be found online under that name
(2026-11-04).

Claims and checks:

- "92 % buy from their first consideration" matches Google/Bain, *B2B Discovery to Devotion*
  (2022). The study surveyed 1,208 US buyers of software, cloud, hardware, telecoms, logistics,
  marketing and industrial equipment: 86 % have a "Day One list" and 92 % buy from it. That is
  enterprise B2B, not small businesses buying packaging.
- "Twice the chance of purchase (initial thought)" — not traced.
- "42 % of the effect within 13 weeks, 58 % after, once the campaign is off" matches Thinkbox,
  *Profit Ability 2* (UK, 2024, with Ebiquity, EssenceMediacom, Gain Theory, Mindshare and
  Wavemaker; £1.8bn of media).
  - 58 % of advertising's total profit comes after the first 13 weeks. The note's "14–24 uger" is
    week 14 to **24 months**, not 24 weeks.
  - Short-term profit ROI was £1.87, and £4.11 including sustained effects.
  - The funder is the UK commercial-TV body and the brands are B2C, so read it as direction, not
    as a figure for Westpack.

The ladder — what each step is, where Westpack stands and what the next step would take — is in
`knowledge/meta-research/07-measurement-ladder.md`:

1. Response (short term)
2. Reported return: ROAS, CAC, ROI, CPA (short term)
3. Brand signals: share of search, relevant traffic, product searches, brand lift study
4. Brand measurement ("hvordan på WP?")
5. MMM: an estimate for the next krone (Meridian; "how long is the tail"; geo holdout, e.g. IT, ES)
6. Test
7. Attribution
8. Brand equity model
9. Forecasting
